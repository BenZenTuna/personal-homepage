Kitchen wall

An old laptop screwed flat to the kitchen wall, lid open, one cable out of the corner, running all day. It turns the house lights on, works out what is for dinner, pulls ten years of photographs off the NAS in the cupboard, and watches the birds nesting on the balcony. This is that same interface with a made-up server behind it — every reading on the page is invented, and nothing here reaches a house.

  • The lights, from the wall. Every room the house has a bulb in, through Home Assistant and the Hue bridge — one tap a room, one tap the lot — so nobody has to find a phone and wait for an app while carrying a hot pan.
  • Dinner, worked out rather than looked up. An AI agent reads the shopping list the house sends it, so it knows roughly what is in the cupboards, and suggests things that can be cooked tonight from what is already there. Choose one and it lays the cooking out as steps, one at a time, big enough to read from across the kitchen with both hands full.
  • It sleeps when the kitchen is empty. The wall is a Home Assistant client like any other: a Hue motion sensor sees somebody walk in and the screen comes back, and when the room has been still a while it goes dark. A lit screen nobody is standing at is a lamp pointed at the ceiling.
  • Everything the house has ever photographed. The pictures and videos live on the NAS in the cupboard, and the wall reaches into them all day — a few hundred pulled across at a time and shown at random, so a decade of holidays and small children keeps surfacing between the forecast and the shopping list. The ♥ keeps one.
  • The birds on the balcony. A camera inside the nest box out there, streamed to the panel. The clip looping in this demo is not that stream: it is AI-generated, a stand-in for what the box will show when the new nesting season comes round.

Looking around this copy

  • Six views along the top — Everything, Cooking, House, Photos, Birds, TV — and the gear beside them rearranges the one you are on.
  • Everything is touch-first. Nothing is smaller than a fingertip and there are no hover states. Any panel takes the whole screen with ⤢, and carries the clock, the weather and the views up with it.
  • Tap the temperature for the week, the hourly strip for the next two days, the clock for timers and world clocks.
  • It answers. Lights toggle, dinners can be chosen, timers run, ingredients tick off onto a shopping list, and a kept photograph stays kept — all of it held in your browser and thrown away by Reset.

You are on a small screen, and this is drawn for a 1920×1080 wall. It still works — turn the phone sideways, or open it on a laptop to see it at the size it was designed for.

The photographs are the house's own; the bird box clip is AI-generated. Everything else — the weather, the rooms, the dinners, the events, the channels — is invented. No live data, no accounts, nothing leaves this page.
